# MIDI implementation (0.6.8)

The engine answers MIDI as a Yamaha XG tone generator does, as far as an
orchestra of physical models can. Each part listens on its own channel (part 1
on channel 1, and so on). The code is `app/orch_cc.c`, and its value laws are in
`app/orch_cc_tables.inc`, generated by `tools/gen_cc_tables.py`.

Every one of the 128 values does something: levels move in even dB steps, times
and rates on a log scale, and choices in equal zones.

## Playing controllers

These are per part and not saved. They are **interpolated between the
values sent**, never stepped. Each new value is reached along a straight line
that takes as long as the sender took since its previous message, between 3
and 50 ms. A wheel or pedal sends a value every few milliseconds, each a step
on its 128-value scale. Joined by lines, the steps become the movement the
hand made, with no zipper. A lone jump, such as a switch or the first message
after a pause, glides over 50 ms.

The settings that act on the sound directly glide too, so turning them fast
from MIDI or from the panel fades instead of stepping every block:

- the effect mixes, and switching an effect on or off: 20 ms from dry to full
  wet;
- the shared resonance (CC71, RESON): 20 ms;
- MASTER: one line across each audio block;
- the delay time: it was already pulled one sample at a time.

| Message | Does | Law, reset value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Note on velocity | how hard the note is played: brighter and louder | even dB over 32 dB in every family (a table per family; 0.6.3 had −63 dB, with the bottom 16 steps nearly silent) |
| Pitch bend | the loop's length, so the pitch | ±2 semitones; RPN 0 sets 0..24 |
| CC1 modulation | vibrato on the part's own LFO (rate CC76, delay CC78), on top of the part's LFO setting | 0..±50 cents, reset 0 |
| CC2 breath | how hard it is bowed or blown: level (even dB over 30 dB) and drive (half to full, so softer is darker) | 127 |
| CC7 volume | part level | GM2/XG curve, 40·log10(v/127) dB; **power-on 100** (−4.2 dB) |
| CC10 pan | balance on the part's seat (the stage places it; 64 leaves it there) | 64 |
| CC11 expression | part level | GM2/XG curve; 127 |
| CC64 sustain | hold | |
| CC67 soft pedal | −2.5 dB and a little darker while down | off |
| CC74 brightness | the strings' and bores' damping | relative, 64 = the instrument, ±1.5 steps |
| CC120, CC123..127 | all sound off, all notes off, mode messages | |

## Effect sends

The effects belong to the whole hall: every channel's send reaches them. They are
one insert after the mix, not a send per part as on XG. Value 0 switches the
effect off, and 1..127 switch it on at a mix of 1..100 %.

| CC | XG | Here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 91 | reverb send | REVERB (shimmer) |
| 93 | chorus send | CHORUS |
| 94 | variation send | DELAY |
| 95 | — | ENSEMBLE |

## Adaptive playing, ARP and sequencer: the played dynamic

The adaptive layer, the ARP's ear, the sequencer's ear and call-and-response
do not hear the bare velocity. They hear how hard the part is being played:
the velocity, less what CC2 breath, CC11 expression and the soft pedal take
off, in steps on the same even dB line (about 4 steps a dB; expression 64
is about 47 steps). A line swelled with a breath controller therefore reads as a
crescendo: the divisi density, the bow energy, the release, the ARP's chord
clusters and the adaptive sequencer follow it. The voice itself keeps the
velocity, because the controllers already act on its output, so nothing is
taken off twice. CC7 volume is a mixing level, not playing, and does not count.

Everything the ARP and the sequencer do to a velocity is now steps on that
line, so it sounds the same at pp as at ff:

- the 16-step phrase arc is −6.9..+3.2 dB (until 0.6.4 it scaled the velocity,
  which on the even line made it 3 dB deep at pp and 10 dB at ff);
- echoes (phrase repeat) are 5, 12 and 24 dB down;
- grooves and accents were already offsets and stay that way.

## Sequencer controllers

The transport is shared, so every channel reaches it. The lane mute belongs to
the channel's part. The panel takes over the run state on its next scan.
There is no tempo controller: the tempo comes from the MIDI clock (below).

| CC | Does | Range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 85 | run / stop | switch |
| 86 | length | 1..16 steps |
| 87 | this part's lane muted | switch |
| 88 | adaptive sequencer | switch |

## Tempo

The orchestra has one tempo, shared by the sequencer, every ARP and the
delay. It comes from three places, in this order:

1. **MIDI clock, always first.** While ticks arrive, the tempo is the
   clock's, measured beat by beat (24 ticks). A new tempo is taken at once,
   and small jitter is halved away. Neither the playing nor the panel can
   move it then; the panel shows the clock's tempo. Half a second without a
   tick ends the clock, and the tempo stays where the clock left it.
2. **Otherwise the playing.** The time between the notes played in is
   folded into the octave of the beat, so eighths and halves count as the
   same pulse. Chords count once, and the ARP's and the sequencer's own notes
   do not count. When two intervals in a row agree within 12 %, the tempo
   moves a quarter of the way towards them. A steady line takes it along;
   a single stretched note or rubato bends it only a little. A rest of more
   than 2 s starts over.
3. **At power on: 128 BPM.** The panel's TEMPO sets it too when no clock
   runs, and the playing carries on from there.
